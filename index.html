for Offline Reinforcement Learning

Abstract

Recent progress in offline reinforcement learning has been driven by expressive generative actors such as diffusion and flow-matching policies, which capture multi-modal behavior in offline datasets. However, these actors require multiple denoising or integration steps per action and thus incur substantial overhead at every decision in deployment. In this work, we revisit where capacity should be invested in an offline actor–critic method. Since the critic is discarded at deployment while the actor runs at every decision step, allocating capacity to the critic is more favorable for inference-time efficiency. We identify three failure modes that arise when critics are deepened—optimization, bootstrap-noise amplification, and value-range drift—and address each with a corresponding ingredient: a residual MLP backbone, n-step bootstrap targets, and a categorical cross-entropy loss. Combining these ingredients with a lightweight deterministic actor, we propose LAC (Light Actor, deep Critic). On OGBench, LAC matches the strongest diffusion- and flow-matching baselines while reducing per-action inference latency by up to 4×.

Key Idea

Asymmetric capacity allocation: The critic shapes the actor during training but is discarded at deployment, while only the actor runs at every decision step. Investing capacity in the critic pays a one-time training cost, whereas investing capacity in the actor pays a recurring inference cost.

Three Failure Modes of Deep Critics

  • F1. Optimization failure — addressed by a residual MLP backbone.
  • F2. Bootstrap-noise amplification — addressed by n-step bootstrap targets.
  • F3. Value-range drift — addressed by a categorical cross-entropy loss.

Key Results

  • LAC achieves the highest average score on the OGBench benchmark suite.
  • LAC-S exceeds all generative-actor baselines using an actor ~1/10 the size.
  • Up to 4× faster per-action inference compared to iterative generative actors.
  • The LAC critic acts as a drop-in improvement for other actor parametrizations.
